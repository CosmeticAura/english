pp. 43 to 46: exposition and inciting incident

Guiding questionWho are the characters? Who is the protagonist? What conflicts do they have within themselves and with each other?
Mr. Kapasi (the protagonist), Mr. and Mrs. Das, and their kids Ronny, Bobby and Tina. Mr. Kapasi is lonely in his marriage and unhappy with his life. Mr. and Mrs. Das bicker and barely act like parents.
p. 43What do the Dases fight about at the beginning of the story?
Who has to take Tina to the restroom. Mrs. Das gives in.
p. 43What does Mr. Kapasi notice when Mrs. Das and Tina walk to the restroom? Why is this important?
She doesn't hold Tina's hand. It shows right away that she's a distant, uncaring mother.
p. 43What does Mr. Kapasi think of how the Dases look? About their marriage?
They look Indian but dress like foreigners, and they seem very young. They act more like siblings than a married couple or parents.
p. 44How does Mr. Das greet Mr. Kapasi?
He squeezes his hand like an American, while Mr. Kapasi greets him the Indian way, palms together.
p. 44How does Mrs. Das greet Mr. Kapasi?
She gives a quick, polite half smile and shows no real interest in him.
p. 45Why are the Dases in India?
Both of them were born in America. Their parents retired to India, so they visit every couple of years, and this time they're sightseeing as tourists.
p. 45Why does Mr. Kapasi find it strange that Mr. Das calls his wife by her first name when talking to Tina?
Indian parents wouldn't do that in front of a child. It shows how American they are and how they act like equals or siblings instead of parents.
p. 46How do you characterize Mrs. Das?
Bored, self absorbed, distant from her husband and kids. She hides behind sunglasses and acts like she'd rather be anywhere else.
p. 46What is the Dases' regular life like? Where? What do they do?
They live in New Jersey. Mr. Das is a science teacher. Mrs. Das stays home with the three kids and is unhappy and bored.

pp. 47 to 52: rising action begins

Guiding questionWho are the characters? What creates tension in the relationships among the characters as the rising action starts?
Mrs. Das ignores her family, Mr. Das hides behind his camera and tour book, and the kids are left on their own. Tension builds when Mrs. Das calls Mr. Kapasi's job romantic and he starts to fall for her.
p. 47What might the "Bombay film magazine written in English" symbolize about Mrs. Das?
She's caught between two cultures: Indian by background but reading about India in English, like a foreigner. It's also her escape from her boring life.
p. 47Why doesn't Mrs. Das stop Tina from playing with the door locks?
She isn't paying attention to her kids. She's wrapped up in herself.
p. 47How do you characterize the monkeys?
Wild, bold and unafraid of people. They look playful but they can be dangerous, which sets up the attack later.
p. 48Why does Mr. Das want to take photos of the hanumans?
He's a tourist collecting pictures. He sees India through his camera instead of really experiencing it.
p. 48Why does Mrs. Das start painting her nails?
To shut everyone out and focus on herself. When Tina wants her nails painted too, she says "Leave me alone."
p. 48What does Mr. Kapasi notice about Bobby in the front seat?
Bobby asks why the steering wheel is on the "wrong" side. Mr. Kapasi explains that in India it's on the right, and that in America it's on the left, something he knows from watching Dallas.
p. 48What is Dallas, the TV show?
An American TV soap opera (1978 to 1991) about the Ewings, a rich and dramatic Texas oil family.
p. 48What impression of the USA might Mr. Kapasi have based on Dallas?
That Americans are rich, glamorous and live dramatic lives.
p. 48How does that compare with his impressions of the Das family?
They're ordinary, careless and childish, not glamorous. But they do have hidden drama, like a soap opera.
p. 49"Mr. and Mrs. Das behaved like an older brother and sister, not parents..." What is the narrative perspective? How do you know?
Third person limited, through Mr. Kapasi. We see the family the way he judges them, and we only get his thoughts.
p. 49Why does Mr. Das want a photograph of the emaciated man and his bullocks?
To him poverty is scenery. He treats real people like a tourist attraction.
p. 50What is Mr. Kapasi's other job? How is it different from translation?
He interprets for a doctor, telling the doctor what patients who speak Gujarati are feeling. Interpreting is spoken and explains meaning. Translating turns written words into another language.
p. 50Why does Mrs. Das call his other job "romantic"?
She's bored, and his job sounds interesting and important to her. She finds it exotic.
p. 51Why does Mr. Kapasi hesitate after Mrs. Das calls his job "neat"?
No one has ever seen his job as special. He sees it as a sign of failure, so her praise surprises him.
p. 51What is a malady?
An illness or disease.
p. 51What does "assiduous" mean?
Careful and hard working.
p. 51Why doesn't Mr. Kapasi regard his job in a positive light?
He once dreamed of being a scholar of languages. He took this job to pay a doctor's bills for his son, who died of typhoid, so it reminds him of loss and failure.
p. 52Why does Mrs. Kapasi not approve of his job?
It reminds her of the son they lost. She dismisses it as being a "doctor's assistant".
p. 52Why does the narrator know so much about Mr. Kapasi?
The story is told in third person limited through him, so we get his memories and thoughts.

Synthesis up to p. 52

Guiding questionWhich characters have tension with which characters? Who is the protagonist?
TableMrs. Das has tension with...
Her husband (a bored, bickering marriage), her kids (she ignores Tina and says "Leave me alone"), and herself (she's unhappy and doesn't feel like a real mother or wife).
TableMr. Das has tension with...
His wife (they argue about the kids and the trip). He's also out of touch with his kids and sees everything through his camera.
TableMr. Kapasi has tension with...
His wife (a loveless marriage, she resents his job) and himself (his failed dreams and his son's death).
TableTina has tension with...
Her mother, because Mrs. Das ignores her and won't paint her nails.
TableRonny has tension with...
His father, because he doesn't listen to him and runs off on his own.
TableBobby has tension with...
Nobody openly yet, but there's a hidden one: Mr. Das isn't his real father.
SynthesisWho do you think the protagonist is? Why?
Mr. Kapasi. The story follows his thoughts, and he's the one who changes.
SynthesisWhat is his internal conflict?
His loneliness and his crush on Mrs. Das against the reality that he's married and she barely sees him.
SynthesisWhat is his external conflict?
With Mrs. Das, who uses him for her own needs, and with his wife, who resents him.

pp. 52 to 61: rising action

Guiding questionHow does the tension building among the characters imply their internal and external conflicts?
Mr. Kapasi's fantasy about Mrs. Das grows (internal), while the Das parents keep drifting apart from each other and their kids (external). Each moment of attention from her raises his hopes higher.
p. 53Mr. Kapasi contrasts his wife with Mrs. Das. What are the differences?
Mrs. Kapasi: no interest in his job; the job reminds her of their dead son; their marriage is cold. Mrs. Das: calls his job romantic and asks about it; sees it as exciting; also stuck in a cold marriage.
p. 53Why is Mr. Kapasi so intrigued that Mrs. Das used the word "romantic"? What does this foreshadow?
No one has ever valued his work, and he hears "romantic" as interest in him. It foreshadows his fantasy, and that he's misreading her.
p. 53In what way does Mr. Kapasi believe his marriage is similar to the Das's marriage? What does this mean to him?
Both are loveless and distant. He thinks Mrs. Das is as lonely as he is, so they might understand each other.
p. 53Why does Mr. Kapasi continue to tell stories about his job to Mrs. Das?
He wants to keep her attention. For once he feels interesting and important.
p. 54What conflict is developing for Mr. Kapasi?
Internal: his attraction to Mrs. Das and his dream of a connection, against the reality that he's married and she's a tourist who barely notices him.
p. 54Identify a quote on pp. 53 to 54 that illustrates this conflict. Include a citation.
Pick a line from those pages where he enjoys her attention or imagines a closer friendship, and cite it as (Lahiri 53) or (Lahiri 54). Check your book for the exact words.
p. 54In what time period does this story take place?
The late 20th century, around the 1990s. The book came out in 1999, and Dallas is in reruns.
p. 55Describe Mr. Kapasi's fantasy.
He and Mrs. Das would write letters for years. He'd explain India to her and she'd share her life in America, a friendship across countries.
p. 55Why does Mr. Kapasi "believe that all was right with the world, that all struggles were rewarded, that all of life's mistakes made sense in the end"?
Her interest makes him feel that his losses, his son and his failed dreams were worth it, because they led him to this moment with her.
p. 56 and 57What symbolism might the Sun Temple and its imagery reveal about the Das family? About Mr. Kapasi?
The temple is a chariot for the sun god Surya, covered with carvings of lovers. For the Dases it shows the passion and closeness missing from their marriage. For Mr. Kapasi it shows the love he's never had.
p. 56 and 57What does Mr. Kapasi realize seeing the temple this time?
Looking at the carvings, he realizes his own marriage has never had real intimacy. He has never even seen his wife fully undressed.
p. 56 and 57Why is this realization significant to him?
It shows how empty his marriage is, which makes his fantasy about Mrs. Das even stronger.
p. 58Why does Mrs. Das "[walk] past her children as if they were strangers"?
She feels no real connection to them. She's absorbed in her own unhappiness.
p. 58What does Mr. Kapasi want Mrs. Das to understand about the Sun Temple and about India?
Its beauty and meaning. He wants to be the one who explains India to her and shares what he knows.
p. 59Why does the thought of Mrs. Kapasi's silence "now [oppress] him"?
After feeling listened to by Mrs. Das, he notices how empty and silent his own marriage is.
p. 59What are monastic dwellings? Draw a sketch of them.
Small rooms carved into the rock of the hills at Udayagiri and Khandagiri, where monks once lived. Sketch: a hillside with rows of square doorways cut into the stone.
p. 60Who are the Jains?
Followers of Jainism, an Indian religion built on nonviolence toward all living things. Jain monks lived in these hill caves.
p. 60Why won't Mrs. Das get out of the car?
She says she's tired, but really she wants to talk to Mr. Kapasi alone.
p. 61What is a defile, the noun?
A narrow pass or gorge between hills.
p. 61What does Mrs. Das mean that it's not surprising that Bobby is brave?
A hint that Bobby isn't Mr. Das's son, so he doesn't take after him.
p. 61Why does Mr. Kapasi feel "a prickle on his skin"?
He senses something big and uncomfortable is coming: her confession.

pp. 62 to 68: climax and ending

Guiding questionWhat is the climax of this story? How do the conflicts get resolved? How do the characters change as a result of the climax?
The climax is Mrs. Das's confession and Mr. Kapasi's question, "Is it really pain you feel, Mrs. Das, or is it guilt?" His crush ends and he sees the family clearly. The Dases don't change at all.
pp. 61 to 631. What does Mrs. Das tell Mr. Kapasi?
That Bobby isn't Mr. Das's son. Bobby's father is a friend of Mr. Das who stayed with them in New Jersey.
pp. 61 to 632. Why has Mrs. Das kept her secret for so long?
Guilt, and fear of breaking up her family. She's never told anyone in 8 years.
pp. 61 to 633. How would you characterize the Das's marriage? Use the Tone List.
Distant, cold, bored, indifferent. They married young and act more like roommates or siblings.
pp. 61 to 634. How do you think Raj Das feels in his marriage? Why?
Content and clueless. He has no idea anything is wrong and is happy with his photos and tour book.
pp. 61 to 635. How do you think Mina Das feels in her marriage? Why?
Trapped and lonely. She married young, feels buried by her kids, and carries a secret she can't share.
pp. 64 to 656. How does Mr. Kapasi react to Mrs. Das's confession? Why is he disturbed by what she tells him?
He's shocked and insulted. She sees him as an older father figure who can fix her, not as someone she's interested in.
pp. 64 to 657. What does Mrs. Das want from Mr. Kapasi? Why does she confess to him specifically?
She wants him to "interpret" her pain and make her feel better, like he does for the doctor's patients. She picks him because he's a stranger with that job.
pp. 64 to 658. How does Mr. Kapasi interpret Mrs. Das's feelings about her secret? Why does he say this?
As guilt, not pain: "Is it really pain you feel, Mrs. Das, or is it guilt?" He says it because it's true, and because he's hurt.
pp. 64 to 659. How does this moment resolve his internal conflict about Mrs. Das? How have his emotions changed?
His crush ends. He goes from hopeful and excited to disappointed and cold, and he feels he was "not even important enough to be properly insulted."
pp. 66 to 6710. Why does Mr. Kapasi want to alert Mrs. Das about the monkeys?
She's dropping puffed rice, and the monkeys follow food. Even after she hurt him, he still feels responsible for the family's safety.
pp. 66 to 6711. What is the climax, when Mrs. Das's conflict erupts? How does Mr. Kapasi react? Why does she tell him her secret?
Her confession and his guilt question. She glares and storms off. She told him hoping he could take away her guilt.
pp. 66 to 6712. How does Mrs. Das's confession resolve Mr. Kapasi's internal conflict? How have his emotions changed?
It kills his fantasy. He no longer hopes for letters or friendship. His hope turns into disappointment.
pp. 66 to 6713. How might the Dases' inability to deal with the monkeys be symbolic of their relationship with India and their heritage?
They can't handle the monkeys, just as they don't really understand India or their own heritage. They're outsiders in their parents' country.
p. 6814. Why is this "the picture of the Das family he would preserve forever in his mind"?
All he keeps is a memory, not a friendship. The address slip blowing away ends his hope, and his mental picture mirrors Mr. Das's camera.
p. 6815. Is this a happy ending?
No. Bobby is safe, but no one really changes, the secret stays hidden, and Mr. Kapasi loses his hope.

Third level: theme and global issues

Guiding questionChoose 3 abstract words, write a thematic statement, and connect to a global issue.
ThemeChoose 3 words from the abstract word list.
Loneliness, communication, guilt. Also good: identity, isolation, longing.
ThemeCraft a 3rd level thematic statement. This is like life because...
People can feel most alone inside their closest relationships, and sharing a language with someone doesn't mean they truly understand you.
Global issuesWhich categories does the text represent?
Culture, Identity, and Community (Indian Americans who are tourists in their parents' country), and Beliefs, Values, and Education (what marriage and family should mean).
Global issuesWhat specific global issue do you see? Fill in the blanks.
The tension between Indian heritage and American identity. The impact of loneliness on marriage and family. Communication as a source of misunderstanding.
Global issuesWhere do we see it locally?
Families in NYC who visit their parents' home country and feel like outsiders there.
